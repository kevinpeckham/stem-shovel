# Looper

**Looper** in the Tools menu (and the footer) is a loop station that anyone can use, signed in or not: a loop of one to eight bars at a tempo plays round and round while you record layers onto it one pass at a time, from the microphone, the piano or the drum machine. Lay down a beat, add a bass line on the piano, sing over it, and save the whole thing to the Idea Recorder as a take with each layer as its own stem.

## The loop

The looper is a device like the drum machine and the piano: a screen with the loop's position, the transport, the source, three settings menus and the volume, with the layers below. Set the **tempo** (the slider, or **Tap** it in beside it), the **bars** (1, 2, 4 or 8) and the **beats per bar** (4 or 3) in the **Timing** menu before the first layer; once a layer exists they are fixed until you **Clear** the loop. The tempo is shared with the drum machine and the metronome: change it on either, or load a drum preset or a generated beat with a tempo of its own, and the loop follows; once the loop has layers, the drum machine is held to the loop's tempo instead. **Count in a bar before the first pass** gives you a bar of clicks before recording starts; the **click** can sound on the count-in only, through the loop, or not at all. Both are in the Timing menu.

**Play** runs the loop (an empty loop runs its transport so you can record the first layer against the click); **Stop** halts everything. The space bar plays and stops the loop unless the drum machine or the piano panel was the last thing you touched, in which case it works that instrument.

## Recording a layer

Choose the source under **Input Source**: the **microphone** (it asks for permission the first time), a **line in** (a second input, for an instrument on an audio interface), the **computer** (audio from another program), the **piano** or the **drums**, each with its own level meter; choosing the piano or the drums opens that instrument if it is not out already.

Each source but the drums has a small menu button beside it. The microphone's and the line in's choose the device from what your computer has and how it is taken: stereo, or one channel on both sides, which is what an instrument on channel 1 of a stereo interface wants. **Computer** captures another program through the browser's share picker: pick a tab, a window or the screen and tick "Share audio". Chrome and Edge share a tab's audio anywhere and the whole computer's audio on Windows; on a Mac, only a browser tab's audio can be shared, so route another program through a loopback device such as BlackHole and choose that as the line in instead; Safari cannot share audio. A slider in the computer's menu shifts its layers earlier by the capture's own delay. Each of the three has a **Gain** slider right under its button, −12 to +24 dB, applied as you play, so the meter and the recording carry it: an interface's line input usually sits well under a microphone's level and wants a boost. The piano and the drums have their own volume under theirs, the same one their panels move. A source's meter runs from the moment it is chosen, so you can check a level before recording. **Normalize recorded layers** scales each layer from these inputs so its loudest point sits just under full scale, leaving a near-silent one alone. Press **Record**: from the next bar 1 (after the count-in when the loop was stopped) every full pass of the loop becomes a layer. The button stays lit while recording; press it again and the pass under way is the last layer. **Stop** halts the loop and drops a pass still recording. A layer appears when its pass ends, so with the default two-bar loop and a count-in the first one lands at the end of the third bar after you press Record; the status line counts it down. Recording the drums starts the drum machine's beat on bar 1 (after the count-in when the loop was stopped) at the loop's tempo, restarting it in step if it was already playing; open the drum machine from the toolbar to choose the beat first. The piano records whatever you play on its panel, keys or MIDI. A loop holds up to 16 layers.

Each layer has a level, a mute and a solo, a waveform with the playhead, and a delete button; **Undo** removes the last layer.

## The inputs' latency

A sung or played layer reaches the computer a little late (the microphone's and the speakers' round trip, usually 10 to 60 ms, more over Bluetooth), which would make it sit behind the beat. The looper shifts microphone and line-in layers earlier by the **Input latency** setting in the microphone's or the line in's menu. Press **Calibrate** there with the speakers on: three clicks play and the microphone measures how late they arrive; the result is remembered in this browser, and the slider nudges it. The drum machine's beat runs on the loop's own clock and needs none. The piano is different: what you play by hand is timed against the loop as you hear it, which your audio output delays a little (the piano's menu shows how much your device reports; Bluetooth adds a lot), so piano layers are shifted earlier by that amount unless you untick **Shift piano layers by the output latency** there. **Hear the microphone through the speakers** is off by default, since a laptop's speakers would feed back into its microphone; use headphones if you turn it on.

## Save, load and export

Your loop stays in this browser as you work, so a reload or a visit days later brings it back ("back from last time" on the screen); to keep it for good, **Save** it, which needs an account. The loop's name sits above the screen, like the idea's title in the recorder: a placeholder names it until you type one, and you can change it any time. The **Save** menu has how many passes the saved mix should run and three buttons: **Save** stores the loop with every layer, and once saved, saves it again in place; **Save as new loop** keeps the saved one and starts a separate one; **Export to the Idea Recorder** lists the loop among your ideas in the recorder, where its layers can go to a song as stems. A saved loop that was not exported lives in the looper only: the Idea Recorder keeps it out of its list unless you tick **Show loops** there. The screen shows the loop's name and whether it has changed since the save. Saving renders the layers' mix (one, two or four passes long, your choice) and saves it to the Idea Recorder as a new idea's take, with each layer as one of the take's sources, named for what it was. In the recorder, the take's menu then offers **Add N stems to song…** to put the layers on a song as separate stems, and **Add as demo…** adds the mix. The loop's tempo, length and layer settings are kept with the idea. New ideas are filed under the account you are currently working in. The **Load** menu lists every loop you saved, newest first; choose one and it comes back into the looper, layers, tempo and bars, replacing what is there (export that first if you want to keep it). Below the loops, **Import a take** lists your recent Idea Recorder takes: a multitrack take comes in as one layer per source, a stereo take as one layer. With an empty loop the loop's length can follow the take (rounded to 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars at the current tempo); otherwise the take is fitted to the loop's length. Set a start offset for a take that begins before its downbeat. A take only sits on the beat if it was played to the tempo.

## Notes and panels

A loop has a note board under the looper, exactly as an idea has in the Idea Recorder: lyrics, chords, what to try next; it saves as you type, and comes back with the loop. On a desktop, the looper and the notes each have a button in their header to pop out into a panel you can drag and resize, and the same button puts them back.
