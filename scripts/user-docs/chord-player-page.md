# Chord Player

The piano's sounds on a circle of fifths: press a chord to hear it, hold it as long as you like, and find the chords that go together side by side.

## How to use the Chord Player

Press any wedge of the circle and the chord sounds for as long as you hold it; the outer ring is the major chords, the inner ring their relative minors, and the chords beside any chord are the ones that fit it best. Hold the 7 pad, or Shift, for a seventh. Turn the circle with the Key buttons to your song's key. The Sound menu, the presets and the Effects menu are the piano's: a grand piano, an electric piano, an organ and three synths, with reverb, delay, chorus, fuzz and the rest.

Switch Jot on and the pad under the circle writes down what you play: each chord lasting until the next, a long silence a rest. Play it back with a click, loop it, tap a chord on the pad to change its length or drop it, save it as MIDI, and, signed in, keep it in your account by name with a note board under the device that saves as you type. Set the tempo, or tap it, in the Timing menu, and turn its click on to jot in time.

### Quick Tips

- Play from the computer keyboard: the number row plays the majors round the circle from your key, the row below it the minors, and Shift adds the seventh; or switch the UI menu to the map by degree, where 1, 4, 5 is I–IV–V. The keyboard button shows each wedge's key.
- Notes mode turns the circle into twelve single notes, for a melody over a chord you remember.
- Style puts the sevenths on the buttons: Blues for dominant sevenths everywhere, Jazz for maj7, m7 and m7♭5 by degree, Lush for ninths and thirteenths. Its Voicing spreads them: spread or rich for a fuller sound, bass for a low root.
- The Strum button staggers the notes as a hand would; it suits the grand piano.
- Space is the sustain pedal and Escape stops everything, as on the piano.
- The arch draws the key and its neighbours big and runs the far keys straight on from its ends; the UI menu's Layout brings back the whole circle; with the key at the bottom (the UI menu) it turns over for a thumb on a phone.
- The IV button writes each chord's Roman numeral in your key on its wedge, and the UI menu can dim the chords outside the key, so a progression like I, V, vi, IV is there to see.
- On the pad a chord lasts until the next one starts; to write a rest, leave two beats or more of silence, or tap a chord on the pad and make it one.
