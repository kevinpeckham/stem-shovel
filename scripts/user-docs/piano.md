# The Piano

**Piano** in the Tools menu (or **/piano**) is a piano and synth in your browser: play it from the screen, from the computer keyboard, or from a MIDI keyboard. It works without an account.

## Playing

On a phone, tap **On** first: it wakes the audio so the first note is not late (on a computer the first key does this by itself). **Off** silences the piano.

Tap or click the keys; slide across them for a run; on a touch screen, several fingers play several notes at once. The lower on a key you press, the louder the note. On a phone the keys stand on end, running down the screen with the low notes at the bottom and the black keys along the left; only as many keys show as fit at a comfortable size (about an octave and a half), and the octave buttons or the arrow keys move the window. Press nearer the right edge for a louder note.

On the computer keyboard the bottom row is the first octave: **Z X C V B N M** are the white keys from C, with the sharps on the row above them (**S D G H J**), and the row from **Q** plays the octave above, with its sharps on the number row. The letters are printed on the keys; the small keyboard button beside the octave hides them. The **arrow keys** move the keyboard an octave up or down (or use the − and + beside the octave), the **space bar** is the sustain pedal while you hold it (**Sustain** switches it on to stay), and **Esc** stops everything.

## Sounds

The **metronome** button in the More row opens a small menu: start or stop it (the button lights while it runs and the icon swells on the downbeat), set the tempo, or **Tap** it in; the screen shows the tempo and the beat while it runs. On a phone it is a section of the sliders menu, which now opens one section at a time: Metronome, Presets, Volume, Effects and More.

**Presets** keep a sound and its effects under a name: five buttons numbered 1 to 5 hold the site's starting presets, and the screen shows the name of the one you are on (with "edited" once you move a slider). Click a button to load its preset. To save the sound you have, ⌘-click (Ctrl-click on Windows) or press and hold a button, give it a name and save; signed out, it lives in this browser, and signed in, it goes to your account where every member can use it. The bookmark button beside the slots opens the full list: save without taking a slot, search, rename, move a preset to a button, delete, or copy a link that opens the piano with that preset for anyone. On a phone the presets are a section of the sliders menu.

**Sound** chooses between a Grand Piano (a sampled concert grand), an Electric Piano, an Organ, a Synth Lead, a Pad and a Pluck. The Grand Piano arrives in stages: a first set of samples in a moment (the screen says "loading the piano…" until then), then a fuller set with four velocity layers in the background on the piano page, and, if you press **Hi-res**, a lossless set with six layers and the sound of each key's release, about 72 MB once; your browser keeps it for next time and the piano remembers the choice. **Volume** is yours to set, and **Effects** opens the reverb (its level and the size of the room), a delay (its level, time and feedback, with a choice of clean Digital repeats or Analog ones that darken and soften as they go, with a slow tape-like wobble), a fuzz (drive and tone), a wah (Touch opens the filter with how hard you play, Sweep moves it on its own; range and resonance shape it, and a MIDI mod wheel or expression pedal rides it while you use one), a chorus (mix, rate and depth), a phaser or a flanger (one or the other, with mix, rate and depth), a tremolo (depth, rate, and Smooth or Chop) and a rotary speaker (Off, Slow or Fast, the rotors spinning up and down between them); the button lights while any of them is up; the piano remembers the sound, the octave, the levels and the effects in your browser.

## Key and chords

**Key** lights a scale on the keyboard: choose a root and a scale (major, minor, harmonic minor, the pentatonics, blues, dorian, mixolydian, lydian) and the keys in it carry a mark, the root a yellow one, while the keys outside it go a shade darker; **1–7** numbers the keys by their degree in place of the letters. The screen names what you hold: a note, an interval ("a fifth"), or the chord (C, Am7, Gsus4, F♯dim, with an inversion as a slash chord, C/E), and in a key the chord's Roman numeral too, so G B D in C major reads "G · V". None takes the key away. On a phone the key lives in the levels menu.

## A MIDI keyboard

Plug a MIDI keyboard or pad in and press **Connect MIDI**: the browser asks once, and from then on the controller plays the piano, with its velocity and its sustain pedal. This works in Chrome and Edge; other browsers do not offer MIDI to web pages yet.

The Grand Piano is the [Salamander Grand Piano](https://github.com/sfzinstruments/SalamanderGrandPiano), Alexander Holm's recordings of a Yamaha C5, shared under the Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 license, in the subset published by Tone.js. Thank you.
