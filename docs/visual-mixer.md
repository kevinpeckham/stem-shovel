# Visual mixing: the Stage view (design draft)

Status: researched and planned 2026-10-16, nothing built. Kevin's ask:
"toggle between traditional track view and visual mixer view for stem
player… a graph-like visualization where the x axis is pan and y axis is
some expression of RMS… background visualization shows combined level
and pan content of the mix… a way to visualize apparent size / width of
a stem… mute and solo in this view… move the track icon to affect level
and pan… later: reverb, compression… long names truncated, track numbers
visible, standardized tags so vocal tracks group and match colors, tags
auto-generated by an LLM, ML or an algorithm… infrastructure re-usable
across Dawn and the stem player." Bias: start simple, enrich release by
release.

The name in the app is **Stage** (the toggle reads Tracks / Stage): a
stage is what the precedents call it, it says left-to-right placement
without saying "mixer" twice, and it is one short word for a button.

## What the precedents do

- **SANDYNE's Visual Mixer** (sandyne.com/#mixer): the closest to the
  ask and the simplest. Every track is a numbered puck on a 2D stage,
  "horizontal pan and vertical volume axes… drag left/right for pan,
  up/down for volume… mixing by feel, not by number". Ghost trails show
  where each puck has moved over time; a master strip and loudness meters
  sit at the right. Nothing about frequency or width.
- **VisiStage V2** (visistage.com): a 3D stage where depth is distance
  (attenuation, air absorption, early-reflection timing). Each track's
  icon is "a live 128-band spectrum of what that track is playing right
  now; a translucent ghost holds the peaks"; crest factor flashes,
  saturation colors, phase rotation twists the shape. Mute, solo, pan,
  depth, width per track; VCA groups with relative offsets; an eight-band
  EQ, compressor, gate and saturation per track; room parameters.
- **Limbus Spatial Stage** (limbusaudio.com): a front-view 3D stage,
  horizontal is pan, vertical is depth, and level follows the position.
  Each object shows "its own live spectrum… with the color identity of
  its track"; a double-click opens a full analyzer. Modifier keys lock a
  drag to one of pan or level. Multiple objects move as a group
  "conserving their relative separation". Air absorption, early and late
  reflections grow with depth; a 5-band EQ and dynamics per sender.

The pattern across all three: a puck per track with its number and
color, pan on x, a level-like quantity on y, a drag that writes two
parameters at once with a modifier to lock one, a live spectrum as the
puck's shape once the product matures, and groups that move together.
Depth, reverb and EQ come with a processing chain the stem player does
not have (Dawn has effects per track, so "depth" lands there first, see
Phase 4).

## What we already have

The engine side is mostly built; the Stage is a new view over it.

- **Controls.** `StemEngine.setGain / setPan / toggleMute / toggleSolo /
  setMaster` and the Studio engine's `setGain / setPan / setTint`, both
  reactive (`$state`). `MixControls` in `src/lib/audio/localMix.ts`
  already names exactly the slice a stage needs (stems with gain, pan,
  muted, soloed, plus the setters), and `localMix` keeps a listener's
  own mix in the browser, so dragging pucks persists the same way the
  knobs do; members save a default mix as they do now.
- **Live levels.** `engine.levels` and `masterLevel` (`MeterState`,
  `src/lib/utils/meterLevel.ts`) per frame, from an AnalyserNode per stem
  buffered and from the mixer worklet streamed (`takePeaks`); the Studio
  has the same shape. They carry peaks, not RMS, see Phase 1.
- **Static loudness, for free.** Every stem row stores `peaks` (max-abs
  per bin across the whole file, `chunkPeaks.ts`), computed at upload.
  The mean of the bins above a silence floor is a usable loudness
  proxy for placing a puck before any analysis job exists.
- **Tints.** `STEM_TINTS` (eight restrained hues, `stem.tint`, shared
  with the song) and the Studio's `track.colour` on the same palette. A
  puck's color is its tint; tags give a default tint (Phase 3).
- **Knob.** The popover readout idiom (value shown while a control
  moves, double-click resets) carries over to a puck.
- **ffmpeg on the server.** The jobs function already runs
  `ffmpeg-static` 7.0 for renditions; its `ebur128`, `astats`,
  `aspectralstats` and `aphasemeter` filters give integrated loudness,
  RMS, spectral centroid / spread / rolloff and stereo phase per stem in
  one pass (Phase 2), no ML.
- **A listening model.** `aiDetect.ts`'s `listen()` already sends audio
  (the mix rendition) to Gemini through the AI Gateway with a valibot
  schema for the answer and an `ai_request` log row; tagging is the same
  call with a different question (Phase 3).
- **Layout.** Container queries (`@container` on the player), never
  window width; the stem player's header already holds the master
  controls where a Tracks / Stage toggle goes.

## The shape of the Stage

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ L                            C                            R  │  louder
 │                                                              │   ↑
 │               ( 3 )                   ( 1 )                  │
 │              Keys                    Lead vox                │
 │     (5)                   (2)                                │
 │    Gtr L                 Bass                   (6)          │
 │                                                Gtr R         │
 │                          ( 4 )                               │
 │                          Drums                               │
 │                                                              │   ↓
 │ ──────────────── L ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│▓▓▓▓▓ R ────────── master ▓▓▓▓░ │  quieter
 ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 2 - Bass   M  S  ⋯    −3 dB   L 10        [Tracks] [Stage]   │  selected strip
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **x is pan**, −1 at the left edge to +1 at the right, a faint center
  line, L / C / R marks along the top.
- **y is level in dB**, the fader's range as the meters draw it (−48 dB
  at the bottom, the fader's top at the top), so a puck's height reads
  the same as the meter's length. What "level" means is the first
  decision below.
- **A puck per track**: a circle with the track number inside, in the
  track's tint (the player's blue when untinted), the name below it
  truncated by CSS (`max-width` and an ellipsis, the full name in a
  `title`, as the rows do). Muted: hollow and dim. Soloed: a bright
  ring. Un-soloed while another is soloed: dim, as the rows go.
- **A live halo** round each puck grows with the track's level this
  frame (from `engine.levels`), so the stage breathes while playing
  and a silent track sits still.
- **The bottom edge**: a left / right balance bar (the sum of each
  track's live level through its pan weights, the same law the worklet
  uses, `panWeights`), and the master meter.
- **The selected strip** under the stage: the puck last clicked or
  focused, with its number, name, M, S, the row's ⋯ menu (the same
  snippet the rows get), and the level and pan readouts. It is how
  mute, solo and the menu stay one click away without crowding the
  pucks, and where a keyboard user reads the numbers.

Interaction:

- **Drag** a puck: x writes `setPan`, y writes `setGain`, both live; the
  readouts follow in the strip and in a small popover by the puck (the
  Knob's idiom). **Shift** locks the drag to the axis it started
  along (Limbus's lock, with one key instead of two). **Double-click**
  returns the pan to center (as the pan knob), **Alt / Option
  double-click** the level to unity.
- **Keyboard**: a puck is a `button`; arrow keys nudge it (1 dB, 0.05
  pan; with Shift 3 dB, 0.2), M and S toggle mute and solo as in a row,
  Escape clears the selection. Each puck announces "3, Keys: −6 dB, L
  20" through `aria-label`; the strip is the readout.
- **Touch**: pucks are 44 px targets; a drag is the same gesture; the
  strip's M / S are the taps.
- **Overlap**: pucks draw quieter-on-top so a small puck is never buried
  under a loud one, and the selected puck always on top; names hide for
  pucks within a name's width of a louder neighbor once the stage holds
  more than about twelve tracks (container-query driven, so a phone
  hides earlier).

What the Stage does not do: it is not a second mix. It drives the same
engine state the rows drive, so switching views never changes a sound,
and the listener's local mix and the saved default mix work as now.

## The first decision: what y means

Kevin: "some expression of RMS". Three readings, and the choice shapes
the whole view:

1. **y is the fader.** Sandyne's choice. Honest as a control, but every
   track at unity sits on one line whatever it sounds like, and a quiet
   recording at +6 dB sits above a hot one at 0 dB that is in fact
   louder. The stage then shows settings, not the mix.
2. **y is the live RMS.** The truth per frame, but a puck that jumps
   with the music cannot be dragged, and a silent track falls off the
   bottom.
3. **y is the heard level: the fader plus the track's own loudness.**
   The puck rests where the track sounds in the mix (a hot stem at
   unity sits high, a quiet one low), dragging adds fader gain on top,
   and the live RMS is the halo, not the position.

Recommendation: 3, from the first release, with the loudness term from
the stored `peaks` (the mean of the bins above −60 dB, relative to the
loudest stem in the song, so the loudest at unity sits at 0 dB) until
Phase 2 replaces it with ffmpeg's integrated loudness. The mapping is
one pure function (`src/lib/utils/stagePosition.ts`: `gain`, `loudness`
→ y; y, `loudness` → `gain`), so the fallback to reading 1 is a flag,
and the view explains itself with one line of help text ("Height is how
loud the track sounds; drag up and down to change its level").

## Phases

### Phase 1: the Stage view (one release)

The toggle and the pucks, on the existing engine, nothing new on the
server.

- `src/lib/components/MixerStage.svelte`: the stage, the strip and the
  toggle's state, over a `MixerModel` (below). Pucks are DOM buttons
  positioned by percentages inside a `@container` box with a fixed
  aspect (3:2, min-height 20 rem; 4:3 under 40 rem wide). The halos,
  the balance bar and later the field draw on one canvas behind the
  pucks, redrawn by the same frame loop that drives the meters (it
  already stops when the meters rest).
- `src/lib/audio/mixerModel.ts`: the interface a stage drives,
  `MixControls` plus `levels`, `masterLevel`, and per track `number`,
  `label`, `tint`, `loudness` (dB), `width` (0..1, Phase 2) and `tag`
  (Phase 3); with `stemMixerModel(engine)` and `studioMixerModel(studio)`
  adapters. The stem player and Dawn share the component through it.
- `src/lib/utils/stagePosition.ts` (pan / dB ↔ x / y, nudge steps),
  `src/lib/utils/stemLoudness.ts` (the `peaks` proxy), both with tests;
  `src/lib/utils/panBalance.ts` (live L / R from levels and pans, the
  worklet's law).
- `StemPlayer.svelte`: `view: "tracks" | "stage"`, a Tracks / Stage
  segmented control in the header, remembered per browser
  (`localStorage`, `stemshovel.playerView`); the stage replaces the rows,
  the section and comment timelines stay below at full width (they are
  on the rows' grid today; the stage view gives them the whole width).
  The ⋯ menu snippet and the badge snippet pass through to the strip.
  The home page demo gets the toggle too, since it is the same
  component.
- RMS: the worklet's `takePeaks` adds a sum of squares per stem and the
  sum (one multiply-add in the loop it already runs), buffered mode
  computes it in `#meterStep` from the samples it already reads, and
  `MeterState` gains `rms` (smoothed, 300 ms). The halo uses RMS; the
  bars keep peaks.
- Dawn: `StudioTimeline` gets the same toggle; stage view hides the
  lanes and shows the stage over `studioMixerModel`, the track menu in
  the strip. If it does not fit the release, it is the first item of
  Phase 2 and the adapter still ships in Phase 1 so nothing is designed
  twice.
- Tests: the utils; a component test with a fake model (a drag writes
  gain and pan, Shift locks, keys nudge, M / S toggle, the strip
  follows the selection); a browser test on the dev song that toggles,
  drags puck 1 and reads `window.__stems` back.
- Docs: `docs/stem-engine.md` "Stage" section, the player's user doc
  (`stems-and-playback`), the Dawn guide.

### Phase 2: analysis, size and the field (one release)

What a track is, measured once at upload, so the stage can show size
and width and the mix's weight.

- **An `analysis` job** beside `mix` and `notes` in `src/lib/server/jobs.ts`
  (`scheduleAnalysis(stemIds)`, posted after a stem's upload completes
  and after a replace), run in the jobs function: one ffmpeg pass per
  stem over the playback rendition,
  `-af ebur128=peak=true,astats=measure_overall=RMS_level+Peak_level+Crest_factor,aspectralstats=measure=centroid+spread+rolloff+flatness,aphasemeter=video=0`
  parsed from the log into `{ lufs, rms, peak, crest, centroid, spread,
  rolloff, flatness, phase }`, stored on a new `stem.analysis` JSON
  column (one additive migration; the Studio's sources get the same
  column on `studio_source`). Deterministic, about a second a stem,
  no model.
- **Puck size** from loudness (integrated LUFS relative to the song's
  loudest, replacing the `peaks` proxy for y as well) and **puck width**
  from two things Kevin named: stereo width for a stereo stem
  (`aphasemeter`'s mean phase: 1 is mono, 0 is wide, below 0 is out of
  phase, drawn as a warning tint) and the frequency extent (a low
  spectral centroid reads wider, since bass is felt everywhere; the
  spread stretches it further). The puck becomes an ellipse whose
  width is the width term and whose height is the loudness term,
  clamped so a puck stays a puck (1× to 2.5× the base size).
- **The field**: the "combined level and pan content" behind the
  pucks, a soft additive glow per track at its position, as wide as its
  width and as bright as its live RMS, blurred on the canvas, so the
  mix's weight shows as a cloud: a bass-heavy center, a wide pad, a
  hole on one side. Redrawn with the halos. An option in the stage's
  small menu turns it off for a cleaner look.
- **Mono / stereo marks** on the puck (a dot for mono, two for stereo),
  since a mono track's pan is a position and a stereo track's a
  balance, and the stage should say which.

### Phase 3: tags, groups and auto-tagging (one release)

- **A tag vocabulary** in `src/lib/constants/stemTags.ts`: vocals,
  backing vocals, drums, percussion, bass, electric guitar, acoustic
  guitar, keys, piano, synth, strings, brass, woodwinds, fx, other. A
  `stem.tag` column and the Studio track's `tag` in `StudioSchema` (one
  additive migration), set from the row's ⋯ menu (a ComboBox over the
  vocabulary) and shown as a small word on the puck's label.
- **Tag colors**: each tag has a default tint from `STEM_TINTS` (vocals
  rose, bass clay, drums slate, guitars sage and olive, keys and piano
  sea, synth lilac, strings sand…) used when the stem has no tint of
  its own, so three vocal tracks match without anyone choosing; an
  explicit tint still wins. The stage draws a legend of the tags in
  use.
- **Groups without subgroups**: Shift-click selects several pucks, a
  click on a legend entry selects a tag's pucks, and a drag moves the
  selection together keeping their relative positions (Limbus); M and
  S on a multi-selection act on all of it. No bus, no shared fader: the
  tracks keep their own gains, which is what "treated as a group to
  some extent" asks for.
- **Auto-tagging, three tiers, cheapest first**:
  1. _Filenames and labels_, deterministic, at upload: a table of
     stems' habitual names (vox, vocal, lead, bv, bgv, kick, snare,
     drums, perc, bass, gtr, guitar, ac, keys, pno, piano, synth, pad,
     str, horns…) in `src/lib/utils/tagFromName.ts`, tested. Most band
     exports name their stems; this alone tags most songs.
  2. _The analysis_, from Phase 2, refines a miss: a mono stem with a
     centroid under 200 Hz is bass; a wide stem with a high centroid
     and high flatness is fx or synth; no tag when unsure.
  3. _The listening model_, on a button: "Suggest tags" in the song's
     settings (and in the stage's menu) sends a 20-second excerpt of
     each untagged (or every) stem's rendition to Gemini through the
     existing `listen()` with the vocabulary and a schema, logged to
     `ai_request` like key detection, and fills the tags, each one
     editable. Opt-in because it costs and takes a few seconds, and
     because a wrong tag is cheaper to prevent than to spot.
     A tag the user set by hand is never overwritten by any tier.

### Phase 4: shapes and the later vectors (as wanted)

- **Live spectrum silhouettes**: the puck's face as a small 16-band
  spectrum of what the track plays now (VisiStage and Limbus). Buffered
  mode has an AnalyserNode per stem already (`getByteFrequencyData`);
  streamed mode needs the worklet to post band energies (a 16-band
  filterbank or a small FFT per 1024 samples), the only new audio work
  in the phase. Container-query gated: at phone width the puck stays a
  circle.
- **Reverb and depth** land in Dawn first, where a track has a send:
  depth as a blur and a size drop with the send amount, the puck
  drifting "back" (a lighter, softer disc), since the stage's y is
  taken by level. The stem player gets it only if it grows a per-stem
  send (open question, not planned).
- **Compression** as VisiStage does it: the crest factor from the
  analysis as the puck's "firmness" (a hard-edged disc for a squashed
  track, a soft one for a dynamic one), and in Dawn a flash when the
  track's compressor reduces gain.
- **Ghost trails** (Sandyne): where a puck has been during this
  session, fading, as a way to see what has been fiddled with.

## Decisions (recommendations in brackets)

1. What y means: fader, live RMS, or heard level? [Heard level: the
   fader plus the track's own loudness, the `peaks` proxy until the
   analysis job exists; the live RMS is the halo.]
2. Where the stage sits: replace the rows and keep the timelines below,
   or a side-by-side with the rows? [Replace the rows; the timelines
   take the full width. Side by side is for Phase 4 and wide screens, if
   ever.]
3. Mute, solo and the menu: on every puck, or in the selected strip?
   [The strip, plus M / S keys on a focused puck; a puck stays a clean
   disc and a phone stays usable.]
4. Dawn in Phase 1 or Phase 2? [Phase 1 if the adapter is as thin as
   it looks; otherwise the first item of Phase 2. The component is
   shared either way.]
5. Auto-tagging: automatic at upload, or on a button? [Filenames and
   analysis automatic and silent; the model on a button, opt-in and
   logged.]
6. The tag vocabulary: the fifteen above? Merge with anything the
   Studio already calls a track role? [Fifteen is enough to color and
   group; the Studio has no role field today, so the same column lands
   in both.]
7. The view memory: per browser, per song, or a member's setting?
   [Per browser, one value for the player everywhere, as the looper
   remembers its layout; the positions themselves are the mix.]

## Costs and risks

- Phase 1 touches the worklet (RMS) and the engine's meter step: small,
  but the stem player is the product; the browser tests for playback
  and meters must stay green and the streamed path needs a look on the
  phone.
- Phase 2's job adds about a second of ffmpeg per stem at upload (in
  the jobs function, never on a page), and one JSON column; a 32-stem
  upload queues 32 jobs, which the function already handles for
  renditions.
- Phase 3's model tier costs per call (Gemini Flash on 20 s of audio
  per stem, cents a song) and is opt-in for that reason.
- The stage is a new way to change the mix; the local-mix "your mix
  differs from the default" affordance and "Save as default mix" must
  show in stage view exactly as in tracks view, or members will not
  know what they changed.
